# The CRAM bridge at order 24

At every primitive 24th root \(\zeta\), Lean proves that the radial limit of Ramanujan’s compensated series \(f(t\zeta)-b(t\zeta)\) is the finite value \(B_{12}(\zeta)=-4U_{12}(\zeta)\) from Folsom, Ono, and Rhoades. At the positive-angle root, the FKTY normalization gives the same value as the two-cutoff shadow period recovered from S6 through degree 852. The equality also holds as the limit of the compensated Taylor packets read from any signed source with the order-24 phase supplied by its lanes. ## From the disk to the boundary

For \(|q|<1\) Watson’s complementary identity is
\[
f(q)-b(q)=-4\psi(-q),\qquad
\psi(-q)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-q)^{n^2}}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1+q^{2j+1})},\qquad
b(q)=\prod_{j\ge1}\frac{1-q^j}{(1+q^j)^2}.
\]
The Lean proof multiplies by the common product \(\prod_{j\ge1}(1+q^j)\). Fine’s rank expansion, Gauss’s alternating-square identity, and the odd and even least-part sums give the three weighted series. The disk product is nonzero and cancels. [Product identities](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/FORRadialIdentity.lean) · [Compensated disk identity](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/FORPsiRadial.lean) · [Watson, *The Final Problem*, p. 63](https://www.mi.uni-koeln.de/NumberTheory/teaching/Seminare/MockTheta/pages/vortragS06.pdf#page=31)

Let \(T_n\) denote the summand including \(T_0=1\). At a primitive 24th root, \(\zeta^{12}=-1\), and every block of 12 successive odd denominator factors has product 2. It follows that \(T_{n+12}(\zeta)=T_n(\zeta)/2\). For \(0<t\le1\), every ratio of successive radial terms is bounded in norm by its endpoint ratio. This follows from \(|1+u v|\ge u |1+v|\) for \(|v|=1\) and \(0\le u\le1\), applied with \(u=t^{2n+1}\). The geometric bound gives dominated convergence and
\[
\lim_{t\uparrow1}\psi(-t\zeta)=2\sum_{n=0}^{11}T_n(\zeta)-1.
\]
To identify the value, write \(D=\prod_{j=0}^{11}(1+q^{2j+1})\) and clear the denominator of the head sum to get \(W=D\sum_{n=0}^{11}T_n\). In \(\mathbb Z[q]/(q^{12}+1)\), the exact coefficient identities are
\[
D-2=2(q^8-q^4+1),\qquad W-1-U_{12}=(2q^2-2q)(q^8-q^4+1).
\]
At a primitive 24th root, the factor \(q^8-q^4+1\) vanishes. The order-8 control also obeys \(q^{12}=-1\) and gives \(D=8\). [Geometric sum and radial limit](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/FORPsiRadial.lean) · [Exact polynomial evaluation](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/BoundaryValues34.lean)

## Signed source and the shadow period

Put \(M=30030\) and take \(r=X\bmod M\) and \(a=X\bmod 8\) from the same signed source. The order-24 quotient requirement is \(24/\gcd(M,24)=4\). The parity of \(a-r\) is even, so the readout can divide it by 2 and use the inverse of \(M/2=15015\) modulo 4. Lean proves
\[
\frac{X-r}{M}\bmod 4=3\left(\frac{a-r}{2}\right)\bmod4,\qquad
X\bmod24=\left(r\bmod24+6\left(3\frac{a-r}{2}\bmod4\right)\right)\bmod24.
\]
The least positive single anchor modulus is 8 and the least compatible packet period is \(\operatorname{lcm}(30030,24)=120120\). With mod 4, \(X=0\) and \(X=60060\) give identical readings and opposite phases at every primitive 24th root. The formulas apply to the actual pair of readings of one source. [Required resolution, readout, and alias witness](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/FOROrder24.lean)

Shifting the two readings by a packet residue \(d\) multiplies its character phase by \(\zeta^d\). The offset from \(X\) factors out of both the finite FOR packets and the radius-weighted Taylor packets. At \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/24}\), removing this offset and applying the common FKTY factor \(\mathrm{fktyScale}\,e^{-2\pi i/576}\) identifies the FOR packets of any cutoff at least 12 with \(\phi_{\rm FKTY}(-1/24)\). The compensated Taylor packets tend to this value along every radial sequence with sufficiently large Taylor cutoffs along it. Both results equal the averaged S6 shadow period with at least 144 frequency slots, supplied by any prefix through degree 852 or higher.
