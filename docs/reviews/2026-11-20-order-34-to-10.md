# The compensated values of f at orders 34 and 10

The positive-angle roots $e(1/34)$ and $e(1/10)$ lie on two radial paths for Ramanujan's third-order $f$. After compensation, their boundary values enter the **same weight-$1/2$ transformation**. Its defect is a period of the unary theta series that occurs in the analytic shadow of $f$. CRAM can evaluate the finite side at both roots. The order-10 query uses the primary period $30030$. The order-34 query needs the quotient class modulo 17.

## From a radial limit to the reflected rational cusp

Write $e(z)=\exp(2\pi i z)$ and $(a;q)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^j)$. For a primitive root $\zeta$ of exact order $2k$, Folsom, Ono and Rhoades (FOR) prove

$$
 B_k(\zeta)=\lim_{t\uparrow1}\big[f(t\zeta)-(-1)^k b(t\zeta)\big]
 =-4\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\zeta^{n+1}(-\zeta;\zeta)_n^2,
 \qquad b(q)=\frac{(q;q)_\infty}{(-q;q)_\infty^2}.
$$

The factor $1+\zeta^k$ kills every later summand. At orders 34 and 10 the half-orders 17 and 5 are both odd, so the interior functions to evaluate are $f(t\zeta)+b(t\zeta)$ in both cases. [FOR, Theorem 1.1](https://afolsom.people.amherst.edu/FolsomOnoRhoades-ForumPi.pdf#page=4)

In *“Strange” combinatorial quantum modular forms*, Folsom, Ki, Truong Vu and Yang (FKTY) use two series, $F(w;q)=\sum_{n\ge0}w^{n+1}(wq;q)_n$ and $U(w;q)=\sum_{n\ge0}q^{n+1}(wq;q)_n(w^{-1}q;q)_n$. Their Corollary 1.3 gives $F(-1;q^{-1})=U(-1;q)$ at every primitive even-order root $q$. Here $-4U(-1;q)=B_k(q)$. The quantum set for their parameters $(a,b,c)=(1,2,0)$ is $S_2=\{h/d:\ d>0,\ \gcd(h,d)=1,\ 2\mid d\}$. In the normalization printed on p. 322, the resulting value at $x=h/d$ is

$$
 \phi_{1,2,0}(x)
 =-4\sqrt{12i}\,e(x/24)F(-1;e(x))
 =\sqrt{12i}\,e(x/24) B_{d/2}(e(-x)).
$$

The inverse nome fixes the sign of the cusp. A positive-angle FOR root $e(1/d)$ contributes to $\phi(-1/d)$. The principal square root fixes the constant phase. FKTY's lower-half-plane function at $z=x-iy$, $y>0$, is the Eichler integral $\vartheta^*(z)=\int_{\bar z}^{i\infty}\vartheta_{1,2,0}(u)(u-z)^{-1/2}\,du$. Its path starts at $x+iy$. They obtain the rational transformation by matching cusp asymptotics as $y\downarrow0$. The radial approach in FOR keeps $t e(-x)$ inside the unit disk. [FKTY, Corollaries 1.3–1.4 and the specialization of (1.9)](https://afolsom.people.amherst.edu/FKTY-JNT16.pdf#page=6) · [FKTY, lower-half-plane integral and proof of Theorem 1.8](https://afolsom.people.amherst.edu/FKTY-JNT16.pdf#page=26)

## The matrix, period, and differential shadow

Take $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\24&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma(24)$ and $x=-1/34$. Then $Ax=x/(24x+1)=-1/10$. Both fractions belong to $S_2$. The pole of this action is $-1/24$, and $24x+1=5/17>0$. The two characters in FKTY's multiplier have denominator 1 and $\epsilon_1=1$, so $\psi_{12}(A)=1$. Their Theorem 1.8, equation (1.13), specializes to

$$
 \sqrt{12i}\left[
 e(-1/816) B_{17}(e(1/34))
 -\sqrt{17/5}\,e(-1/240) B_5(e(1/10))
 \right]
 =\int_{-1/24}^{i\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{1,2,0}(u)}{\sqrt{u+1/34}}\,du .
$$

The period uses the vertical path $u=-1/24+it$, $t>0$. Its principal square root is evaluated at $u-x=-5/408+it$, which approaches the negative real axis from above. In the published theorem the period gives the real-analytic extension of the defect away from the pole. The matrix also appears in FKTY's Example 1, where the authors use a different input cusp, $1/2$. The earlier weight-$3/2$ result of Bryson, Ono, Pitman and Rhoades uses $U(1;q)$. Here the twist is $-1$ and the weight is $1/2$. [FKTY, Theorem 1.8](https://afolsom.people.amherst.edu/FKTY-JNT16.pdf#page=9) · [FKTY, Example 1](https://afolsom.people.amherst.edu/FKTY-JNT16.pdf#page=10) · [Weight-$3/2$ specialization](2026-11-20-quantum-radial.md#a-quantum-modular-form-from-the-same-two-variable-series)

Calling the displayed period $P$, one can solve for the raw order-34 endpoint. The phase difference is $1/816-1/240=-1/340$, giving

$$
 B_{17}(e(1/34))=\frac{e(1/816)}{\sqrt{12i}}P
 +\sqrt{17/5}\,e(-1/340) B_5(e(1/10)).
$$

Thus the order-10 endpoint and the period together determine the order-34 endpoint. An order-10 reading supplies a single residue. Evaluating this formula requires the complete order-10 packet value and independent period data. The phases $e(-1/816)$ and $e(-1/240)$ belong to the specified representatives $-1/34$ and $-1/10$. Replacing $x$ by $x+1$ preserves the nome and multiplies $e(x/24)$ by $e(1/24)$. An observation used to determine the normalization for *varying lifts* requires its own admission. The modulus-17 condition below concerns the universal order-34 residue query.

FKTY's bilateral theta series (3.12) has coefficient $n\chi_{12}(n)(-1)^{(n-1)/2}$ at $e(n^2u/24)$ for odd $n$, with $\chi_{12}(n)=(12/n)$ and all even coefficients zero. The character times the sign is $+1$ at $n\equiv1\pmod6$, $-1$ at $n\equiv5\pmod6$, and zero otherwise. Pairing $n$ and $-n$ gives, for $\operatorname{Im}u>0$,

$$
 \vartheta_{1,2,0}(u)
 =2\Theta(u/24)
 =\tfrac12(\xi_{1/2}M_f)(u/24),\qquad
 \Theta(z)=\sum_{m\equiv1\ (6),\ m\in\mathbb Z}m\,e(m^2 z).
$$

Here $M_f$ is the completed function with holomorphic part $e(-z)f(e(24z))$. The factor two comes from the bilateral pairing, and the factor one half uses the previously proved identity $\xi_{1/2}M_f=4\Theta$. Substitution under the integral uses equality of its integrands at each positive height. Convergence at the endpoint of the improper integral and analytic regularity of the defect come from FKTY's cusp-form argument. ## Which residue data evaluate the endpoints?

Let $X=r+30030K$ with canonical Euclidean remainder $r=X\bmod 30030$. At order 10, $X\bmod10=r\bmod10$. At order 34, changing $K$ by $j$ preserves the target for every source exactly when $34\mid30030j$, or equivalently $17\mid j$. Two sources $r$ and $r+30030$ give the same primary reading and differ by 8 modulo 34. Even a power of $30030$ supplies no factor 17.

An authentic additional reading $s=X\bmod17$ and the primary chart $c=r\bmod17$ give the quotient class on demand. Since $30030\equiv8\pmod{17}$ and $8\cdot15\equiv1\pmod{17}$,

$$
 \kappa=15(s-c)\bmod17=K\bmod17,\qquad
 X\bmod34=((r\bmod34)+8\kappa)\bmod34.
$$

These identities apply to signed $X$. The anchor and chart must refer to the same source and observation. For example, $X=-1$ gives $r=30029$, $s=16$ and $c=7$. Thus $\kappa=16$ and the projection gives $(7+8\cdot16)\bmod34=33$. The retained readings suffice to compute $\kappa$ when queried. For other admitted coupled anchors with quotient resolution $H$, the universal order-34 query is recoverable exactly when $17\mid H$. The least common period with this primary is $510510=17\cdot30030$.

For a finite boundary sum, hold $\zeta$ fixed inside each Pochhammer product and group the summands $n<N$ by their **outer exponent** $n+1\bmod L$. The full array of those packets, paired with the character $r\mapsto\zeta^r$ and scaled by $-4$, evaluates $B_k(\zeta)$ whenever $N\ge k$ and $2k\mid L$. The periods used here are $L=510510$ and $30030$ respectively. The chart certifies or evaluates an individual residue query. Obtaining the full endpoint also requires all of its weighted summands. [Packet definitions](../../formal/universal/QuantumRadial.lean) · [Observation and endpoint theorem statements](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/BoundaryObservation34.lean)

The interior Taylor packets for $f$ give another way to reach this value. Let $t_j\in(0,1)$ tend to 1, $\zeta=e(1/34)$, and $f_N(q)=\sum_{d<N}a_dq^d$. Given the interior Taylor identity and FOR's radial limit, there are degree cutoffs $K_j\ge j$ such that, for every $N_j\ge K_j$, the sums $f_{N_j}(t_j\zeta)+b(t_j\zeta)$ tend to $B_{17}(\zeta)$. Substituting the solved FKTY formula gives their limit in terms of $B_5$ and $P$. Here $N_j$ counts Taylor degrees. The finite $U$ packets above already stabilize at 17 and 5 summands. ## Exact endpoint polynomials

At a primitive tenth root $z$, the finite product reduces modulo $\Phi_{10}$ to $B_5(z)=4z-4z^2+8z^3$. For a primitive 34th root $z$, write $B_{17}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{15}c_j z^j$. In ascending degree, its coefficients after reduction modulo $\Phi_{34}$ are

$$
(c_0,\ldots,c_{15})=(-4,8,-4,8,-8,12,-8,8,-4,8,-12,12,-8,8,-8,12).
$$

The reflected single-product sum $F(-1;z^{-1})$ and the positive-angle squared-product sum $U(-1;z)$ agree at both roots. Normalization by the separate phases in the transformation applies after this reduction. [Endpoint polynomial and reflection proofs](../../adapters/MockThetaAnalytic/BoundaryValues34.lean)
